# Playbook: Contract Redline

You've been handed a contract — vendor MSA, client SOW, partnership agreement, employment offer, NDA, services agreement, lease, settlement agreement, etc. The counterparty drafted it. Your job: figure out what to push back on, what to ignore, and what's worth pulling counsel into.

This playbook is the universal redline framework. For domain-specific situations (equity, real estate), pair with the relevant playbook.

---

## The 80/20 Redline Principle

A contract has dozens of clauses. You won't redline all of them. Focus on the ones with disproportionate impact:

| Priority | Clause type | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Always review | Payment terms | Direct dollar impact, immediate |
| Always review | Termination | Determines your optionality |
| Always review | Liability / indemnity | Determines your downside risk |
| Always review | IP ownership | Determines what you keep |
| Always review | Confidentiality | Determines what you can talk about |
| Review carefully | Warranties / reps | Determines what you're committing to be true |
| Review carefully | Dispute resolution | Determines where/how fights get resolved |
| Review carefully | Term / renewal | Determines lock-in |
| Review carefully | Assignment / CoC | Determines what happens on acquisition |
| Glance | Notice provisions | Procedural but matters when you need it |
| Glance | Governing law | Affects everything else; harder to negotiate post-signing |
| Glance | Severability / integration | Boilerplate but matters in edge cases |

## The Universal Redline Pass

For any contract the user receives, do this systematic walkthrough:

### Pass 1 — Whose paper is this?

Contracts drafted by the counterparty's lawyer are biased toward the counterparty. That's just how it works. Your default assumption: every ambiguity will resolve against you, every silence will be used against you, every "reasonable" or "standard" claim is probably one-sided.

If at all possible, get to a position where YOUR paper is the starting point. Failing that, redline assertively.

### Pass 2 — The financials

- **Price**: clearly stated? Currency? Tax-inclusive or exclusive? Discounted from what?
- **Payment terms**: when due? Net 30, 60, 90? Late fees? Interest on overdue?
- **Scope of work**: what's in, what's out? Is "additional scope" defined and priced?
- **Change orders**: how are they handled? Mutual written consent? Standard rates?
- **Reimbursable expenses**: any? With caps? Approval thresholds?
- **Refunds / pro-rates**: under what conditions? Mechanics?
- **Currency / payment method**: who bears FX risk? Wire fees?

### Pass 3 — Termination & exit

- **Term length**: how long is the commitment? Auto-renew?
- **Termination for convenience**: do you have one? Notice period? Penalty?
- **Termination for cause**: defined? Cure period? Materiality threshold?
- **What survives termination**: payment obligations, IP, confidentiality, indemnity?
- **Wind-down rights**: data export, knowledge transfer, customer notifications?
- **Renewal mechanics**: notice required to NOT renew? How far in advance?

The most expensive contracts in business are the ones you can't easily exit. Demand a termination-for-convenience clause where you can.

### Pass 4 — Liability & risk

- **Indemnification**: who indemnifies whom for what? Mutual or one-way?
- **Liability cap**: total cap? Per-claim? Excluded categories (IP infringement, gross negligence, etc.)?
- **Liability carve-outs**: what isn't capped? (Typically IP infringement, gross negligence, willful misconduct, confidentiality breach, indemnity obligations.)
- **Consequential damages waiver**: mutual? Watch one-way waivers.
- **Insurance requirements**: what's required? Reasonable to your business size?

Standard structure for service agreements:
- Cap at 12 months of fees paid (or fees paid in some period)
- Mutual waiver of consequential damages
- Standard carve-outs (IP infringement, gross negligence, confidentiality, indemnity)
- Mutual indemnity for IP infringement of your respective contributions

Anything dramatically more aggressive against you is worth pushing back on.

### Pass 5 — IP

- **Pre-existing IP**: does each party retain its pre-existing IP? (Yes.)
- **Work product / deliverables**: who owns what's created during the engagement? (Depends on deal; should be explicit.)
- **License grants**: what is the licensee allowed to do? For how long? Where? Exclusively?
- **Improvements / derivatives**: who owns? Who has rights to use?
- **Feedback / suggestions**: standard clause is "licensor gets all rights to feedback" — usually fine to accept

For service work: usually customer owns deliverables (work product) on full payment; service provider retains pre-existing IP, tools, methods, and aggregated/anonymized learnings.

For licensing: scope of use, exclusivity, sublicensing, term, geography, termination.

### Pass 6 — Confidentiality

- **Mutual or one-way?** Should usually be mutual.
- **Scope of "confidential information"**: defined? Excludes publicly known, independently developed, lawfully obtained from third parties?
- **Duration**: typical 3-5 years post-termination (or perpetual for trade secrets)
- **Permitted disclosures**: to advisors, lawyers, accountants under similar obligations
- **Required disclosures by law**: notice requirement before disclosing
- **Return / destruction of confidential info**: required on termination

### Pass 7 — Reps, warranties, and disclaimers

- **What is the counterparty representing as TRUE?** (Their authority, no conflicts, ownership of IP they're providing, compliance with law)
- **What are YOU being asked to represent?** Anything you can't actually verify? Anything that might be untrue under some interpretation?
- **Disclaimers**: usually "AS IS" and "WITHOUT WARRANTY" language. Make sure you have at least implied warranties (merchantability, fitness for purpose) where appropriate; service providers often disclaim all warranties.
- **Survival**: which reps survive termination?

### Pass 8 — Dispute resolution

- **Governing law**: which jurisdiction? Convenient or hostile to your interests?
- **Venue**: where do disputes get litigated?
- **Mediation/arbitration**: required first? Arbitration body (AAA, JAMS, etc.)? Cost allocation?
- **Class action waiver**: present? Enforceable in jurisdiction?
- **Attorney fees**: prevailing party? Defending party? Each party bears own?
- **Injunctive relief carve-out**: usually IP / confidentiality matters can go to court directly

### Pass 9 — Other operational clauses

- **Force majeure**: scope? Triggers? Notice?
- **Assignment**: can either party assign without consent? Change of control treatment?
- **Subcontracting**: allowed? With consent? Liability for subs?
- **Most-favored-customer / nation**: any? Watch out — can backfire.
- **Audit rights**: any? Reasonable scope and cost allocation?
- **Notices**: where, how, who's noticed? Email allowed?
- **Amendments**: written, signed by both? Email amendments allowed?

### Pass 10 — The "everything else" boilerplate

These rarely become issues but verify:
- **Severability**: contract survives if one clause is void
- **Integration**: this contract supersedes all prior agreements
- **Waiver**: not waiving a right one time doesn't waive forever
- **Headings**: not part of substantive contract
- **Counterparts**: signing in counterparts allowed (multiple signed copies = one contract)

## How to Redline — Mechanics

### Use track changes

In Microsoft Word or Google Docs with suggesting/redlining mode. The counterparty's lawyer can see exactly what you changed.

### Use markups for substantive changes

For each substantive change, leave a comment explaining your basis: "Caps at fees paid because that's market-standard for services agreements."

### Send your redline with a cover note

Don't just send a redlined PDF. Include a cover note summarizing the top 3-5 changes and your reasoning. Makes the counterparty's lawyer's job easier (faster turnaround) and frames the conversation around what matters.

```
Hi [name] —

Thanks for the draft. I've sent back a redline. The substantive changes
are in three areas:

1. Liability cap (section X): capped at 12 months fees paid; this is
   market-standard for services arrangements.
2. Termination (section Y): added termination for convenience with 30
   days notice; the current draft only allows termination for cause.
3. IP (section Z): clarified that I retain pre-existing IP and tools;
   work product transfers on full payment.

Everything else is mostly clarification and clean-up. Happy to walk
through any of it.

[Your name]
```

### Use the counterparty's logic against them

If they're pushing one-sided clauses, point it out diplomatically:

```
"I notice the contract is one-way on [X] — only I'm obligated. To keep
this clean, can we make it mutual? Either both parties have [obligation]
or neither does."

"Section [Y] gives you termination rights for [reasons], but I have no
equivalent. Symmetric termination feels right here — what would your
preferred structure be?"
```

Symmetry is a hard frame to argue against.

### Use objective criteria

"Market standard is [X]" beats "I want [X]." Sources for "market standard":
- Comparable contracts you've signed (in similar deals)
- Industry standard agreements (e.g., NVCA model docs for VC, Bonterms for SaaS)
- Lawyer comments on what's typical
- Public templates (Y Combinator SAFE, FAST advisor agreement, etc.)

### Pick your three battles

You won't win every redline. Decide which 3-5 changes are non-negotiable for you, and which are nice-to-have. Trade gracefully on the nice-to-haves to win the non-negotiables.

## When to Walk Away From a Contract

Some contracts shouldn't be signed at any reasonable redline. Walk if:

1. **Unlimited liability with no caps and no carve-outs.** You're underwriting unbounded risk for capped revenue.
2. **One-way IP grant of your pre-existing work.** You're giving them your toolkit for the price of one deal.
3. **Auto-renew with 90+ day opt-out windows and steep penalty for early term.** You're surrendering optionality.
4. **Governing law / venue chosen to make litigation impossible for you.** Cross-border with venue in counterparty's home court, no arbitration alternative.
5. **Counterparty refuses to redline anything.** "Take it or leave it" on a draft they wrote is a power move. Sometimes legitimate (giant SaaS company). Often a tell about how they'll behave during the relationship.
6. **Disproportionate damages / liquidated damages clauses against you.** Especially watch out in SaaS, licensing, real estate deposits.
7. **Personal guarantees on debts of an entity.** Almost never sign these unless you've deeply considered the implications.

## Tactical Phrases — Common Pushbacks

For frequent vendor-favorable clauses, here are common pushback patterns:

### "Limitation of liability" too low or one-sided

```
"I want to make sure the liability structure is symmetric. The current
draft caps your liability at [X] but leaves mine uncapped. Can we mirror
it — both parties cap at [12 months fees paid / similar amount]? That
keeps the risk symmetric to the deal economics."
```

### Unlimited indemnity from you

```
"Section [X] obligates me to indemnify you for [broad scope]. That's
broader than I can underwrite. Can we narrow it to claims arising from
my actual breach of [specific obligations]? And cap indemnity at the
overall liability cap?"
```

### IP grab on your pre-existing work

```
"The current IP clause sweeps in my pre-existing tools and methods.
That isn't what I'm selling — I'm delivering [work product], which
transfers to you on payment, but my underlying toolkit needs to stay
mine. Can we adjust the scope?"
```

### Auto-renewal with short opt-out

```
"The 30-day opt-out before auto-renewal puts a lot of pressure on me
to track the calendar. Can we extend the opt-out window to 60-90 days,
or alternatively make the renewal opt-IN instead of opt-OUT?"
```

### One-way confidentiality

```
"Confidentiality should be mutual here — I'll learn things about your
business and you'll learn things about mine. Can we make the obligation
symmetric?"
```

### Restrictive non-compete

```
"The non-compete is broader than what's enforceable in [jurisdiction]
and broader than necessary to protect your interests. Can we narrow it
to [specific defined market / time period / scope]?"
```

### Termination only for cause

```
"I notice there's no termination-for-convenience option. For a [duration]
engagement, both of us probably want an exit ramp if things change. Can
we add a mutual 30-day termination for convenience?"
```

### Mandatory arbitration in their home venue

```
"The arbitration venue makes it impractical for me to enforce. Can we
either move to [neutral venue] or carve out injunctive relief to courts
of competent jurisdiction?"
```

## When to escalate to counsel

You can redline most standard contracts yourself with the framework above. Escalate to counsel when:

- Deal value > $250K
- Contract longer than ~25 pages of substantive terms
- Equity / securities involved
- Cross-border with multi-jurisdiction implications
- IP licensing where the IP is your core asset
- Liquidated damages / penalties that you don't fully understand
- Regulatory implications (GDPR, HIPAA, etc.)
- Any non-standard clause you can't translate into plain English
- You're signing a personal guarantee of any kind
- Counterparty is publicly traded / sophisticated and their lawyer is heavily lawyered

## Quick reference: redlines by deal type

### Vendor MSA (you're the customer)
- Cap liability at 12 months fees paid
- Mutual confidentiality
- Termination for convenience with 30-day notice
- No auto-renewal OR 60+ day opt-out window
- IP infringement indemnity from vendor
- Data ownership stays with you; vendor disclaims rights to your data

### Client SOW (you're the vendor)
- Cap liability at fees paid for the relevant statement of work
- Mutual indemnity scoped to your respective contributions
- Pre-existing IP retained; deliverables transfer on full payment
- Right to use general learnings and aggregated insights
- Payment terms: net 15-30; late fees; interest on overdue
- Termination: notice required; payment for work performed through termination

### NDA
- Mutual
- Reasonable scope of confidential info
- Standard exclusions (publicly known, independently developed, lawfully obtained)
- Term: 3-5 years (or perpetual for trade secrets)
- No assignment to competitors
- Return/destruction on termination

### Employment / Contractor (you're the employee/contractor)
- Reasonable non-compete (geographic, temporal, scope-limited)
- Inventions assignment limited to work product (not outside / pre-existing work)
- Equity vesting clearly defined with acceleration triggers
- Severance terms / good-leaver/bad-leaver
- Confidentiality scope reasonable
- Garden leave provisions explicit

### Real estate (see real-estate.md for full detail)
- Title / encumbrance review
- Closing cost allocation explicit
- Inspection / due-diligence contingencies with adequate windows
- Earnest money / deposit forfeit conditions
- Closing date + remedies for delay
- Handover punch list mechanism

### Equity / term sheet (see equity-valuation.md for full detail)
- 1x non-participating liquidation preference
- Broad-based weighted average anti-dilution
- Founder vesting with double-trigger acceleration
- Reasonable board composition (don't lose control)
- Protective provisions scoped to major actions
- No-shop limited to 30-45 days

## The final review

Before signing or sending back final redline, do the "would I be okay if this contract were tested in court?" walkthrough:

1. Have I read every paragraph?
2. Can I explain every defined term in plain English?
3. Have I modeled the worst-case scenario under this contract?
4. Have I considered what happens if the counterparty goes bankrupt / disappears / gets acquired?
5. Do I have an exit ramp I can actually use?
6. Are there any clauses I don't fully understand? (If yes, get counsel.)
7. Am I about to sign something just because I'm tired of negotiating? (If yes, pause.)

If anything fails, fix it before signing. Once it's signed, the contract is the deal.
