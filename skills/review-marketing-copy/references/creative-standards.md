# Creative Standards

## Simplify correctly

Make copy as complex as necessary and as simple as possible. Measure simplicity by the audience's interpretation cost, not by word count.

Avoid deleting distinctions an informed audience needs. Technical specificity can make copy simpler for a technical reader because it names the threat, mechanism, and consequence precisely.

Weak simplification:

> **Security made simple**  
> Protect your business with powerful security.

Useful specificity:

> **Stop lateral movement before one compromised identity becomes a breach**  
> Enforce application-level access using identity, device posture, and continuous authorization instead of network location.

Use progressive disclosure. Lead with the simplest truthful promise, then reveal necessary mechanism, detail, and proof.

## Earn cleverness

Write the direct version first. Attempt wordplay only after the proposition is sound.

Keep clever copy when it:

- reinforces the promise or mechanism;
- arises from the product, audience, category, occasion, or action;
- resolves on first reading or rewards a quick second reading;
- fits the brand's demonstrated or supplied voice;
- remains strategically strong after the joke is removed.

Reject it when it obscures the offer, creates the wrong interpretation, trivializes a serious decision, mimics a fashionable challenger voice, or could be used unchanged by competitors.

Generic wordplay:

> **Bank on better.**

Product-rooted wordplay for automatic tax allocation:

> **Save for tax without taxing your attention.**

## Infer voice cautiously

Infer voice from brand guidelines, existing work, audience, product, category, and context. When no clear voice exists:

1. provide the clear strategic version;
2. offer more characterful alternatives separately;
3. label bolder work as a tonal departure;
4. explain the trait being tested, such as dry confidence, warmth, irreverence, or urgency.

Do not confuse tone with voice. Keep the underlying character stable while adjusting tone for the moment.

## Learn from exemplary brands without imitation

Borrow decision principles, not surface style.

- Learn reduction, product truth, human benefit, and visual-verbal discipline from Apple.
- Learn identity, tension, movement, participation, and emotional compression from Nike.
- Learn conviction and values-backed claims from Patagonia.
- Learn contextual play and audience recognition from Spotify.
- Learn intelligent compression and double meanings from The Economist.
- Learn controlled irreverence and category disruption from brands such as Oatly or Liquid Death.

Never ask, `How would Apple write this?` Ask, `What would ruthless prioritization and product truth require here?` Do not recreate recognizable slogans, sentence patterns, campaign devices, or a living brand's voice.

## Protect claim integrity

Classify language before strengthening it:

- **Verified factual claim:** Retain or sharpen only within supplied evidence.
- **Unverified factual claim:** Flag and request substantiation.
- **Comparative or superlative claim:** Require an explicit comparison basis and evidence.
- **Expressive brand language:** Allow imagination if a reasonable reader will not interpret it as a material fact.
- **Aspirational statement:** Keep it distinguishable from a guaranteed outcome.

Strengthen unsupported claims through specificity of mechanism, not invented magnitude.

Unsafe:

> **Cut project delivery time in half.**

Safer mechanism-led version:

> **Move from brief to approval without chasing feedback across five tools.**

Proof-dependent structure:

> **Take projects from brief to approval `[verified outcome]` faster.**

Flag unsupported language such as `the fastest`, `the only`, `trusted by leading teams`, `built for everyone`, guaranteed results, fake scarcity, and invented testimonials.

## Check craft

Prefer:

- concrete nouns and active verbs;
- specific contrasts;
- varied sentence length and deliberate rhythm;
- customer language over internal terminology;
- implications the reader can picture;
- confident statements supported by truth.

Remove:

- throat-clearing and filler;
- stacked abstractions;
- empty intensifiers;
- false grandeur and default futurism;
- repeated benefits disguised by synonyms;
- jargon that signals sophistication without adding precision;
- rhetorical questions with obvious answers;
- puns that need explanation.
