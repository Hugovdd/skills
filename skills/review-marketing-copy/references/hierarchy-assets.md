# Hierarchy and Asset Structures

## Translate appearance into semantic roles

Do not assume that the largest text is functioning as a headline or that every supplied title deserves to remain. Assign the intended job first.

| Element | Job | Common failure |
|---|---|---|
| Eyebrow or kicker | Frame audience, category, occasion, or context | Repeats the section label or wastes the first words |
| Primary headline or H1 | Deliver the dominant promise, tension, or idea | Uses grand but interchangeable category language |
| Supporting headline or deck | Make the main idea concrete, credible, or actionable | Paraphrases the H1 |
| Section headline | Advance the argument with a new reason to care | Merely names a topic such as `Features` |
| Subsection or card title | Communicate one scannable benefit, capability, or proof point | Uses empty fragments such as `Save time` |
| Body copy | Explain the mechanism, implication, evidence, or nuance | Buries the point or repeats the title |
| Proof line | Reduce doubt with relevant evidence | Uses vague social proof without a source |
| CTA | Express the appropriate next action and sometimes its value | Defaults to `Learn more` or demands too much commitment |
| Microcopy | Remove local uncertainty or friction | Introduces unrelated selling points |

Require every level to add information. A supporting line may explain the headline, but it must not merely restate it.

Example of repetition:

> **Work smarter with AI**  
> Transform the way your team works using artificial intelligence.

Example of progression:

> **Resolve the ticket before it becomes a thread**  
> Draft accurate replies from your help centre, product history, and previous resolutions, directly inside the queue.

## Landing pages and websites

Build a persuasive sequence appropriate to awareness rather than enforcing a rigid template. Common roles include:

1. orientation and dominant promise;
2. problem recognition or desired future;
3. mechanism or product demonstration;
4. differentiated benefits;
5. evidence and objection handling;
6. offer and next action.

Check message match between the traffic source and the hero. Check whether the CTA asks for a commitment proportionate to the audience's confidence.

## Video and audio scripts

Treat temporal hierarchy as seriously as visual hierarchy:

1. **Hook:** Earn the next few seconds through relevance, tension, surprise, or recognition.
2. **Premise:** Establish what the story is about before ambiguity becomes confusion.
3. **Escalation:** Develop stakes, contrast, or curiosity.
4. **Reveal or payoff:** Deliver the promised insight, product, or emotional turn.
5. **Proof:** Make the claim credible where the format requires it.
6. **Action or resolution:** Leave the audience with a clear next thought or step.

Review spoken rhythm, breath, natural emphasis, on-screen text, visual dependency, and whether the script merely narrates what viewers can already see. Do not write visual copy as if it were a landing page read aloud.

## Ads and social posts

Distinguish the hook's job from the offer's job. Preserve message match after the click. Avoid curiosity gaps that attract the wrong audience or make the brand feel manipulative.

Check whether the format requires:

- immediate audience recognition;
- a single arresting contrast;
- a legible product or category cue;
- one idea per execution;
- a CTA that completes rather than repeats the message.

## Email

Treat sender name, subject line, preview text, opening, body, and CTA as one hierarchy. Prevent the subject and preview text from repeating each other. Match the opening to the relationship and the recipient's existing context. Optimize for the email's next step, not for explaining the entire product.

## Presentations and sales assets

Make slide titles state the takeaway rather than the topic. Use the sequence of titles as a standalone argument. Let body copy, charts, and evidence substantiate each title rather than compete with it.

## Recommend structural changes clearly

Label recommendations as:

- **Required:** The argument or comprehension fails without the change.
- **Optional:** The current structure works, but the alternative may improve character or momentum.
- **Design-dependent:** The copy recommendation relies on layout, interaction, timing, imagery, or motion that must be confirmed.
