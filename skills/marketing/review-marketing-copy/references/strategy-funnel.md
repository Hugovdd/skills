# Strategy, Audience, and Funnel

## Reconstruct the strategic brief

Express the assignment in one line:

> Convince `[specific audience]`, who currently believes `[current belief]`, that `[offer]` is the best path to `[desired outcome]` because `[differentiating mechanism or proof]`, then move them to `[next action]`.

Treat missing fields as diagnostic findings. Do not fill them with generic marketing language.

## Understand the audience

Identify more than demographics. Infer or establish:

- situation and triggering moment;
- desired progress;
- anxieties, objections, and perceived risk;
- existing alternatives, including doing nothing;
- category fluency and technical vocabulary;
- status, identity, or worldview at stake;
- evidence they trust;
- words they use for the problem and outcome.

Distinguish the buyer, user, approver, and beneficiary when they differ. Do not address all of them in the same sentence unless the asset genuinely serves a mixed audience.

## Classify awareness

Use the dominant audience state, while recognizing that an asset may move readers through adjacent states.

| Audience state | What they know | Primary copy job |
|---|---|---|
| Unaware | Neither problem nor solution is salient | Create recognition, curiosity, or emotional identification |
| Problem-aware | They feel the problem but do not know the path | Articulate the tension precisely and make change desirable |
| Solution-aware | They know approaches exist | Establish why this mechanism or category is preferable |
| Product-aware | They know the offer and alternatives | Differentiate, prove, and resolve objections |
| Most aware | They are close to acting | Reinforce value, confidence, urgency when real, and the next step |

Flag mismatches. Product detail often fails with unaware audiences. A long problem narrative can frustrate product-aware buyers. A clever mystery can harm high-intent traffic that needs confidence and specifics.

## Identify the asset's job

Do not optimize every asset for an immediate sale.

- Use acquisition ads to earn relevant attention and the next click.
- Use homepages to establish position, relevance, and a path for mixed visitors.
- Use landing pages to sustain message match and convert a defined audience.
- Use product pages to make value concrete and reduce product-specific doubt.
- Use pricing pages to support comparison, confidence, and commitment.
- Use launch films to create emotion, memory, and cultural meaning.
- Use retargeting to address known objections or renew desire.
- Use lifecycle email to move an existing relationship one appropriate step.

When brand building and immediate conversion compete, state the trade-off. Choose according to the asset's job and funnel stage while preventing serious damage to either.

## Diagnose the argument

Find these components:

1. **Tension:** What unsatisfactory reality creates movement?
2. **Promise:** What valuable change is offered?
3. **Mechanism:** How does the offer create that change?
4. **Difference:** Why choose this over alternatives?
5. **Proof:** Why believe it?
6. **Action:** What should happen next?

Not every asset needs all six explicitly, but the underlying logic must hold. Challenge positioning when the copy sells a vague category benefit rather than the customer's actual desired progress.

Example:

> `Collaboration software that helps teams achieve more.`

Diagnose it as category-level language with no audience, tension, mechanism, or difference. If the product prevents status meetings by making ownership and progress visible, consider positioning it around operational clarity rather than generic collaboration.
