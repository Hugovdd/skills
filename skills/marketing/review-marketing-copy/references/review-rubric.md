# Review Rubric and Deliverables

## Full review format

Use the sections that materially help. Do not add ceremony to a small request.

### 1. Strategic read

State:

- audience;
- awareness and funnel stage;
- asset and its job;
- intended promise;
- differentiator or missing difference;
- desired action;
- assumptions and missing decisive context.

### 2. Executive verdict

Lead with the outcome. Identify what works, what most weakens the copy, and the highest-leverage change. Avoid vague praise.

### 3. Argument and hierarchy audit

Explain the job currently performed by each major element. Identify repetition, missing steps, misplaced proof, competing ideas, and inappropriate calls to action. Mark structural recommendations as required, optional, or design-dependent.

### 4. Line-level review

Use a compact table when several exact mappings are useful:

| Original | Diagnosis | Revision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|

Keep diagnoses specific. Do not replace every line simply to demonstrate activity.

### 5. Rewritten asset

Present the recommended copy in its intended order and context. Include relevant labels such as eyebrow, H1, deck, scene, voice-over, on-screen text, proof, and CTA only when they aid implementation.

### 6. Alternative directions

When exploration is useful, provide up to three conceptually distinct directions:

- **Clear:** Immediate comprehension and low interpretation cost.
- **Distinctive:** Ownable language and stronger brand character.
- **Bold:** A deliberate strategic or tonal departure.

Name the governing idea and trade-off for each. Do not present synonym swaps as creative territories.

### 7. Integrity check

List claims requiring evidence, assumptions requiring confirmation, and any recommendation dependent on design, timing, imagery, or interaction.

## Evaluation dimensions

Score only when comparison or prioritization benefits from it. Use a 1 to 5 scale and support every score with evidence.

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Clarity | Can the intended audience understand the point at the required speed? |
| Relevance | Does it reflect a real desire, tension, or decision for this audience? |
| Differentiation | Could a direct competitor say the same thing unchanged? |
| Specificity | Does it name concrete value, mechanism, consequence, or proof? |
| Credibility | Are claims proportionate to evidence and audience skepticism? |
| Hierarchy | Does each level perform a distinct job and advance the argument? |
| Emotional force | Does it create an appropriate feeling, identity, or momentum? |
| Memorability | Is there a compact, ownable idea worth remembering? |
| Brand fit | Does it sound native to this brand and moment? |
| Action | Does it make the appropriate next step clear and desirable? |

Do not average scores into a false universal quality number. Weight dimensions according to the asset's job and funnel stage.

## Quick review format

For a headline or short passage, return:

1. **Verdict:** One or two direct sentences.
2. **Assumption:** State only what materially affects the advice.
3. **Options:** Provide a small number of meaningfully different revisions.
4. **Decisive question:** Ask one question only if its answer would change the direction.

Example:

> `Build without limits` has energy but no proprietary meaning. It could describe software, finance, toys, or construction. Assuming this is a developer platform differentiated by deployment flexibility:
>
> - Deploy the stack you actually chose.
> - Your architecture. Your rules.
> - Build freely. Ship anywhere.
>
> Decisive question: which specific limitation does the product remove?
